The one that worries me most

The permission rule is applied in three places in the whole app. There's a proper rule that decides whether someone may open a job — _gcCanSeeProject — and almost nothing consults it. That's why the overdue bar was showing Glenn the company's $347,020 balance.

Blocking one screen at a time is how this keeps happening. It needs to be applied once, at the point where data is handed to the screen, so every page inherits it.

05What I'd do, in this order

Ordered by what protects you first and what earns the most. Each step is small enough to verify before the next one starts.

  1. Apply the permission rule once, everywhere

    Filter data where it's handed to the screen rather than page by page, so every PM sees only their jobs and only office and admin see company money. This is the one with real exposure — Glenn is currently seeing figures he shouldn't.

    protects you · touches one layer · testable

  2. Profit and loss per job

    Contract, minus MS tax, minus overhead, minus materials, labour, subs and supplier bills — the pieces all already exist and are already being added up for commission. This assembles them into one number per job and a total for the company.

    the biggest gap against QuickBooks · reuses working code

  3. Wire up what's already built

    The commission screens, the cheques-to-write screen, the office manager's home, sub pay terms and the insurance tag all exist and work — they just have no button. This is the cheapest value in the whole list.

    low risk · no new logic

  4. Close the automatic gaps in the chain

    Won estimate becomes a job. Approved bill moves onto the pay list by itself. Cheque posts to the books when it's written. Each one removes a click you currently have to remember.

    one link at a time, verified between each

  5. Money we owe, aged — and a chart of accounts

    Once P&L is in, these are the remaining pieces an accountant would ask for at year end.

    last · needed for tax time, not for Friday

How this gets done without breaking things

One change at a time. Tests around that area first, then the change, then the full suite of 27, then the packaged file booted in a real browser and checked — it doesn't get sent unless that passes. That check already exists and already caught a fault nobody knew about: React was being loaded from an outside server with no backup, which would have made the whole CRM a blank page if that server ever went down.

The 45 older feature blocks stay untouched. They work, and nothing tests them yet.
